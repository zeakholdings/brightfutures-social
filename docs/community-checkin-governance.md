# Community Check-In operational governance

**Status: adopted internal BrightFutures HQ operating model (Model B)**

**Operator and data controller:** BrightFutures HQ  
**Independent operators:** Arshan and April

This document records BrightFutures HQ’s operating decisions for the Community
Check-In, Member Highlights and “You said / We did” workflows. BrightFutures HQ
does not claim to be a Greenwich Students’ Union (GSU) or University service.
These decisions have not been presented as approved by GSU or the University.

## 1. Submission review ownership

- Only Arshan and April may access raw `community_checkins`.
- Arshan is the primary reviewer. April is the deputy reviewer.
- Each uses an individual Directus account. Accounts, passwords and tokens must
  not be shared.
- New private check-ins are reviewed at least twice each week during term time.
  April covers review when Arshan is unavailable.
- Review notes must be brief and factual. Private text must not be copied into
  general committee documents, messages or public-content records.
- Other authorised editors may manage `member_highlights` and
  `community_actions`, but must not receive access to private check-ins or their
  source material.

### Access changes

- Keep an internal access register recording the account, access granted,
  reason, start date and end date.
- Remove access and invalidate individual credentials when Arshan or April no
  longer holds the reviewer responsibility. Do this as part of the role handover,
  not after it.
- Review Directus access at handover and at least annually. Public-content
  editors must remain separated from raw check-in access unless BrightFutures HQ
  formally changes and records this operating model.

## 2. Safeguarding, escalation and signposting

The Check-In is not continuously monitored and is not counselling, casework,
crisis support or an investigation service. BrightFutures HQ does not use
automated risk scoring. Arshan or April reads the member’s words in context,
avoids diagnosis, records only necessary facts and does not investigate sensitive
disclosures or allegations.

The current external contact routes still need to be inserted and kept current:

- `[INSERT VERIFIED IMMEDIATE-EMERGENCY ROUTE]`
- `[INSERT VERIFIED SAFEGUARDING/ESCALATION ROUTE]`
- `[INSERT VERIFIED WELLBEING SIGNPOSTING ROUTES]`

### Immediate emergency

This includes a clear indication of immediate danger, imminent self-harm or
suicide, or an immediate threat to another person.

1. Do not investigate, provide counselling or attempt to manage the emergency
   through the form.
2. Use the verified immediate-emergency route without delay.
3. Share only the information necessary for the escalation.
4. Keep a short factual record of what prompted the escalation, when it happened
   and which route was used.

### Serious but not immediate concern

This includes self-harm or suicidal thoughts without stated immediacy, abuse,
serious distress, or an allegation that may present a safeguarding concern.

1. Use the verified safeguarding/escalation route promptly for guidance.
2. Do not contact the person alleged against, decide whether an allegation is
   substantiated or request a detailed account unless the verified route advises
   that step.
3. Restrict identifying information to Arshan, April and the minimum people
   required by the escalation route.
4. Record the referral and outcome factually without conclusions about truth,
   motive or diagnosis.

### General wellbeing concern

This includes loneliness, worry, pressure or distress without an indication of
immediate danger or a serious safeguarding disclosure.

1. If contact details were voluntarily supplied and a reply is appropriate,
   respond briefly within BrightFutures HQ’s community remit.
2. Use only verified, current signposting routes.
3. Do not present BrightFutures HQ as counselling, casework or crisis support.
4. Reclassify and escalate if later information indicates a more serious concern.

### Ordinary feedback

1. Review through the normal check-in process and keep the response private.
2. Create a highlight only through the separate consent and editorial process.
3. Create `community_actions` only through manual, non-identifying interpretation
   of themes.

### Allegations involving another person

- Keep names and identifying details private. Do not reproduce them in public
  content, committee papers or general messages.
- BrightFutures HQ does not determine whether an allegation is substantiated.
- Use the verified safeguarding route to determine appropriate signposting or
  escalation.

## 3. Retention decisions

### Adopted periods

- **Private check-ins:** retain for 12 months after submission, then delete,
  unless a documented safeguarding or legal need requires restricted longer
  retention. Record the reason, restrict access and review the exception rather
  than applying it indefinitely.
- **Published highlights:** review at least annually. Remove a highlight from
  publication when valid consent withdrawal is received.

Deletion includes unnecessary duplicates or exports created for administration.
BrightFutures HQ still needs to record internal retention decisions for consent
audit records, archived highlights, community actions and completed
withdrawal/deletion request records. No period for those records is claimed here.

## 4. Withdrawal, correction and deletion

1. Requests arrive through the published BrightFutures contact route. Arshan
   handles them as primary reviewer; April covers absence.
2. Acknowledge the request without promising immediate deletion and restrict the
   request record to the people handling it.
3. Verify identity proportionately. Prefer matching information already held,
   such as an email supplied with the check-in or the highlight and approximate
   submission context. Do not request student ID, care records, identity
   documents or new sensitive history for a routine request.
4. If identity cannot reasonably be established, ask only for the smallest
   additional detail needed.
5. For a valid website-highlight withdrawal, change the record from `published`
   to an unpublished state and verify that it no longer appears on `/highlights`
   or the homepage.
6. Consider the private source check-in separately. Unpublishing a highlight
   does not silently delete its source; apply the 12-month rule or handle a valid
   deletion request and document the result.
7. If BrightFutures HQ also posted the highlight on social media, use its account
   controls to request or perform removal where it can. Explain that third-party
   shares, screenshots or caches may be outside BrightFutures HQ’s control.
8. Record the request date, proportionate verification, records considered,
   action taken, responsible reviewer and completion date without copying the
   original sensitive text.

## 5. Member highlight publication standard

Manual editorial review is required. Before changing a highlight to
`published`, an authorised editor confirms:

- [ ] Explicit website consent is present and recorded.
- [ ] The exact member-approved public excerpt is used; private answers have not
      been substituted, expanded or paraphrased into the highlight.
- [ ] Attribution matches the member’s selected preference.
- [ ] The excerpt contains no unnecessary sensitive information.
- [ ] The excerpt contains no identifiable third-party information.
- [ ] No unsupported factual claim has been embellished or endorsed.
- [ ] The copy remains a celebration, not a case study or safeguarding account.
- [ ] If an image is used, public use is approved and meaningful alt text exists.
- [ ] No internal source reference or consent-audit field appears in public copy.

Social-media consent is separate from website consent. Website consent does not
authorise social posting, and social consent does not replace the website-consent
requirement for a public website highlight. If any check cannot be completed,
leave the highlight unpublished.

## 6. Data minimisation and sensitive information

- BrightFutures HQ does not actively request health information or other
  special-category data through the Check-In.
- It does not request date of birth, student ID, address, placement history,
  reasons for estrangement, care records or social-worker information.
- A member may nevertheless volunteer sensitive information in a free-text
  response. That information remains private unless the member has separately
  provided an exact approved excerpt and explicit publication consent, followed
  by manual editorial review.
- Reviewers and editors must remove unnecessary sensitive or third-party details
  from consideration for public content. They must not silently rewrite private
  text into an approved excerpt.

## 7. Separation from official membership

Official GSU society membership data is entirely separate from this workflow and
is handled by Greenwich Students’ Union. BrightFutures HQ must not combine
official membership data with `community_checkins`, `member_highlights` or
`community_actions` unless a separate, explicitly approved process is adopted.

## 8. External information to confirm

BrightFutures HQ should confirm only the external facts needed to operate and
describe the service accurately:

1. The current safeguarding, emergency and wellbeing contact routes to which
   BrightFutures HQ may escalate or signpost.
2. Any conditions governing use of GSU/University branding or services.
3. The hosting, Cloudflare and Directus processor/hosting information that must
   be accurately disclosed.

Confirming those facts does not imply that GSU or the University operates or has
approved BrightFutures HQ’s Community Check-In.

## 9. Review record

BrightFutures HQ should record the adoption date, any later amendments and an
annual review date alongside this document. Access, retention exceptions,
safeguarding routes and public-content permissions should be checked during that
